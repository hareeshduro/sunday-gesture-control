03

Gesture protocol

One hand · either hand
    Command colour code Cyan — tracking, rotation off Green — pinched: turning switched on or off Amber — turning, brightness moving

    Hold an open palm to switch on, then turn that same hand: right brightens, left dims. No pinch needed. Turning counts whether you tilt the hand in the air or twist the palm over — both are read. The knob picks up the level the lights are already at, so it never jumps, and about a quarter turn covers the whole range. Pinch once to switch turning on, so it keeps working with your hand in any shape at all; pinch again to switch it off. While the knob is turning, colour commands are held back so a busy hand cannot recolour the room — but a closed fist always switches everything off, locked or not. One brightness is shared by all four colours, so 1-2-3-4 recolours the light without changing how bright it is.

    04

    How to use it

    Start here
    1. 1Link your home. Choose Link home, paste a Home Assistant token, then tick the lights to control. Skip this to try the simulation.
    2. 2Engage optics. Allow the camera. Hold your hand about an arm's length away, palm toward the lens, with the whole hand in frame.
    3. 3Switch on. Hold an open palm up until the command bar fills.
    4. 4Turn that same hand to dim. Right brightens, left dims. Tilt it or twist the palm over — either counts. The number in the middle of your hand follows as you turn.
    5. 5Switch off. Close your hand into a fist and hold it. This always works, whatever else is going on.
    6. 6Pick a colour. Hold up 1, 2, 3 or 4 fingers for red, green, blue or white — the brightness stays exactly where you set it. Pinch once if you want to keep turning the brightness with your hand in some other shape, and pinch again to stop.

    Nothing fires from a glance: every pose has to be held, and an open palm has to actually be turned before it touches the brightness. A pinch can never pick a colour, however your other fingers are arranged, and the hand stays inert for a moment while it unfolds out of one. Four fingers (white) is held longest, because it is one relaxed thumb away from an open palm. Your last colour and brightness are remembered, so lights come back on exactly as you left them. If the room is very dark or lit a strong colour, SUNDAY corrects the camera automatically — watch the Optics readout.
